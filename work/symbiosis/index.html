Symbiosis

Immersive installation concept for the Powerhouse Museum

Symbiosis is an immersive installation inspired by Dresser’s philosophy that true appreciation of beauty arises from contextual knowledge and understanding.

Brief

A university assignment for Visualising Experience at UTS, working with the Powerhouse Museum collection: design an interactive digital experience for one object. I chose Christopher Dresser’s cast‑iron Garden Chair, 1870.

Insight

We see the chair as either a utilitarian object or a piece of art. Reading Dresser’s own books, I found that division reflects design for standardisation rather than an intrinsic truth, and that his ornament is built on botany and geometry.

Response

It uses the Garden Chair to critique the arbitrary homogenisation of everyday objects. Visitors enter through mesh pathways with projections on the walls and floor, and symbols around the chair light up the principles hidden in its design.

Process

  1. 01 CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT · RESEARCH

    I read two of Dresser’s books entirely through and traced the chair in Procreate. My first mind map needed constant reorganising, so I pulled out the figures he references in each chapter and made an annotated mind map of these instead, which allowed me to visually see the connections between ideas and concepts.

  2. DECIDED

    I decided to use modern technologies to reimagine Dresser’s ornamental imagery and engage a contemporary audience with the relationship between science, nature and the arts, creating an ethereal atmosphere without overpowering the concept itself.

  3. CHRISTOPHER DRESSER

    “We have here not only a manifestation of the truth that a principle of order prevails in the development of plants, but also a revelation of the fact that plants are founded upon a geometric basis.” Set in my final render around the chair.

  4. 02 CONCEPT 01 · VR USER JOURNEY

    My initial design was a VR experience where the user could ‘pick up’ a floral motif from the chair and examine it closer.

  5. CREATIVE CODE · WEEKS 5–6

    I coded this gesture in p5.js with ml5 hand tracking and my own Teachable Machine classifier.

  6. DECIDED

    The classifier needed still images while the animation ran on live video, so I used ml5 hand tracking alone.

  7. WHAT IT TAUGHT ME

    Removing unnecessary complexity can lead to better performance.

  8. 03 CONCEPT 02 · REFINEMENT

    A headset keeps the experience to one person, and studio feedback was to focus on the experience, not the technology.

  9. DESIGN CONCEPT 02

    I moved to projection mapping inside sheer walls, with light projected onto fabric so groups explore together.

  10. PROTOTYPE 1 · MATERIALS

    After a wide range of material experimentation, I selected the gutter guard for its structural integrity but adapted my approach due to sharp edges.

  11. 04 TESTING · PROTOTYPES 1–3

    Did the structure guide visitors through at least 2 pathways, in and out?

  12. PROTOTYPE 1 · SPATIAL EXPLORATION & NAVIGATION

    A small-scale model to test how intuitive it is to navigate the pathways. I observed users without interference and recorded the paths they took to the centre.

  13. PROTOTYPE 2 · INTERACTIVE LEARNING & CONCEPTUAL ENGAGEMENT

    Tested whether the symbol interaction is intuitive and whether visitors can derive meaning independently. One user attempted to sit on the symbol, and two reached to touch it.

  14. PROTOTYPE 3 · IMMERSIVE SENSORY EXPERIENCE

    Assessed immersion through passive interaction with movement-tracked evolving projections and natural ambient sounds.

  15. DECIDED · PROTOTYPE 2

    Orientation was the primary requirement, with no ‘correct’ interaction.

  16. WHAT IT TAUGHT ME

    Prototype 2 taught me the necessity of clear interaction cues, and revealed how users interpret design elements in unexpected ways.

  17. 05 FINAL EXPERIENCE · WALKTHROUGH

    I simulated tracking in testing by manually adjusting the projector, so the final concept uses automated motion tracking.

  18. WHAT VISITORS TOOK AWAY · PROTOTYPE 3

    Even without displaying the chair itself, the projections successfully conveyed the intended concepts. When asked what the projection was displaying, there was a unanimous understanding that by the end of the path, the visuals were communicating the geometric complexities behind the final shape.
