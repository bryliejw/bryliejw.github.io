Flip through

Tags are acts of resistance to the increasing standardisation of urban environments.

Brief

A uni assignment for Typography in Context: a magazine for visual communication designers, with masthead, covers and a style guide.

Insight

While organising my photographs into sections, I realised there was a cohesive colour scheme that heavily contrasted against the mundane urban landscape surrounding it.

Response

I colour-graded my photos and masked the backgrounds to enhance this contrast. One spot colour per spread gives the range of street art without clashing, on a grid deliberately broken in key moments, where imposed order coexists with graffiti.

Issue 5 cover artwork, which flows across the front and back cover. Made from my photographs and extended with Photoshop Generative Fill (Adobe Firefly). Graffiti belongs to its writers.

Materials A4 print magazine · typographic style guide · street photography · colour grading · background masking · chisel-marker tag lettering · spray-paint headings · one spot colour per spread · justified grid

Process

  1. 01ISSUE 5 · BACK COVER

    The back cover continues the street scene from the front, with the masthead tag repeated in the orange spot colour above the issue blurb. The masthead is a chisel‑marker tag that changes every issue. For Issue 5 the E becomes an epsilon, the Greek numeral five.

  2. 02STYLE GUIDE · 1.1 MASTHEAD · 3.2 LAYOUT · 4.1 BODY TEXT

    I wrote a style guide that sets the rules for the masthead, type, grid and body text, so each issue can change its masthead and still read as CYPHER. Issue 5 is the reference for every rule.
